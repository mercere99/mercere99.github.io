CSE 452: Organization of Programming Languages returns!

Let’s imagine you write the following program: if (H) then L=5 else L=6; where H is a high-security (unobservable) variable, while L is a low-security (observable) variables. This program has a security bug: an attacker can infer the value of the secret by only observing the value of L. In what language would you write a program that the type checker finds such bugs? Another example is how would one ensure that a set of AI agents do not follow a workflow that leaks secrets of private information?

In CSE 452 you will try out imperative, functional, and verification-aware languages. The course will explore type systems, execution models, LLM-based correctness proofs, especially for security/privacy policies. We recommend it if most of your experience is in one or two languages. Even when you go back to writing C++ or Python, you’ll see choices you didn’t know you were making.

Want an early start on the Computer Science Master’s?

If you’re working on your B.S. in Computer Science or Computer Engineering, shared enrollment can get you to an M.S. in Computer Science within five years total. With advance approval, you can waive up to nine of the 30 required master’s credits for qualifying courses counted toward your B.S. Submit the Request for Shared Enrollment Status form before you begin the first course you hope to share. Email graduate program coordinator Vincent Mattison while you’re choosing your upper-level electives.

Department news: New Master’s Degree in A.I.

You are trying to build a controller to get a robot to walk forward. It figures out that if it stands up tall and then falls on its face it gets credit for forward movement. But how do you get it to actually WALK? If you’d enjoy solving problems like that, take a look at MSU’s newly approved M.S. in Artificial Intelligence. Students build and evaluate systems in areas such as computer vision, machine learning, and natural language processing. Course projects connect theory to practice and explore the ethical questions that arise when people use these systems. An optional thesis gives students room to pursue a research question of their own.

Department news: A proposed bachelor’s in A.I.

We’ve been getting a lot of questions about the CSE department’s proposed major in Artificial Intelligence. The proposal is now under university review, so nothing is final, but we want students to explore what intelligence looks like across a range of methodologies, build AI systems, and learn when and how to use them responsibly in software projects.

If approved, the major could begin in Fall 2027 or (if revisions take longer) 2028. While it is unlikely to be practical for most current students to switch to the new major, we have also added three new AI-heavy courses for CS majors.

Starting next semester, CSE 235 (Tools, Teamwork, and Time) will teach you the basics of working with AI systems on software projects and CSE 345 (Fundamentals of Artificial Intelligence) will give you the basics of how AI systems work and how to build one of your own. Then next Fall we will introduce CSE 336 (Software Engineering II), where you will learn how to responsibly build large-scale software projects with AI support.

Learning to program when A.I. can write code

For some, it can be unsettling to watch an AI tool produce a program in seconds that took you all afternoon; for others it can seem like an easy out to avoid struggling through an assignment. It’s reasonable to wonder what that means for getting your first job. One part of that job (and that interview) we want you to be ready for is the moment something goes wrong, the AI can’t fix it, and tracking down the issue and solving it falls to you. Perhaps the app you’re working on is not responsive enough and the AI keeps swapping out implementations, but they are all too slow. It’s time to go back to your CS fundamentals to reengineer the system.

The key is that even when an AI is doing a lot of the work for you, you need to stay in charge and not give up your agency in the project. If you just outsource your thinking to an AI, what value do you add to the company? The key is to develop an instinct for the fundamentals so that you can exhibit good judgement when you need to. In fact, you can practice that judgment now.

On an assignment where your instructor allows AI, make a real attempt first. When you get stuck, write down what you expected to happen and try the smallest example that surprises you. Now you have a good question to ask a classmate, a TA, or an AI tool. Ask for a hint, try the change yourself, and see if you can explain why it worked. The next day, try a slightly different problem with the chat closed. If you can work through that one, the skill is yours. Merely having the AI explain the solution to you is not enough. Many studies have shown that the more effort you put into learning something, the better you will remember it.

CS Community Notes: Drama over the C++26 standard!

In March, the C++ standards committee voted 114 to 12 to send C++26 forward, with a host of new features and library tools. In October, the draft fell short of the two-thirds support it needed from participating ISO national standards bodies. That was rather unexpected for a language on a regular three-year release cycle. The sticking point was a new feature called contracts.

Suppose you write a binary search that expects a sorted vector. In the proposed design, you could note this precondition in the function declaration. Callers can see what’s required, and the program can check it. But checking that a vector is sorted takes linear time, which would slow your logarithmic search (though only when testing). The main problem that came up is that different developers want different abilities to turn on and off testing and tracing of problems.

It’s disappointing because the ballot covered the whole draft, so the result also held up features such as reflection, which lets a program inspect (and change!) its own types at compile time. A final meeting to update the standard just ended, so hopefully the next vote will go through!
